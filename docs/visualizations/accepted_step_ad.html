How this connects to NEOPAX

This is a toy implicit-Euler cooling solver, not a NEOPAX measurement or Radau implementation. The accepted update solves the cooling equation implicitly. Step doubling estimates its error; a controller chooses the next timestep.

NEOPAX's accepted-step adjoint holds the realized timestep schedule fixed. This animation compares omitting rejected trials from the AD calculation against retaining them with T_next = where(accept, T_trial, T_old). On rejection, the state derivative is the identity and the cotangent into the discarded trial is zero. For this finite, well-behaved example, both gradients agree.

Diffrax's default checkpointed adjoint runs over attempted iterations, while its integrator selects the old physical state and old solver state on rejection. Its PID controller stops gradients through the error-based adaptation factor. See the rejection implementation, the PID implementation, and the adjoint documentation. This toy tape is illustrative; its node counts do not measure Diffrax's compiled work or memory.

Why can a real implementation still differ? A discarded computation can contain a singular or nonfinite derivative; a numerical VJP may then evaluate zero times infinity/NaN. Or auxiliary differentiable state may survive rejection and influence later accepted steps. Neither effect is present in this toy solver, and merely having rejected trials does not prove either problem.

Real NEOPAX rejected attempts can also update numerical caches. That replay issue is absent from this memory-free toy. Accurate implicit stage derivatives require the correct converged stages, not identical Newton iteration counts.

Historical NEOPAX caveat: before commit 0fed61d (June 23, 2026), the attempt replay preserved the plasma state but replaced cached transport/reference data with stopped-gradient rejected-attempt outputs. That is not the unchanged-memory assumption of this animation. See the source comparison and real Kvaerno5 scalar check; the earlier large-gradient result is not explained by this animation alone.

No JAX, VMEC, NTX, transport solve, automatic network request or external library. The two gradients are computed by a small scalar reverse-mode AD engine and checked against finite differences with the same recorded accepted schedule.
